citewatch: a sourcing signal for AI search

Which websites AI search answers cite, how fresh those pages are, and what a publisher can change. Built on an open-source, pre-registered study: 100 UK health questions, 2 models, 2 runs.

What the first study found (run 1 unless noted; both runs pooled for stale):
  • nhs.uk is cited in 83% (83/100; 95% CI 75%–90%) of GPT-6.1 Sol answers and 10% (10/100; 95% CI 5.0%–16%) of Claude Sonnet 5.5's.
  • The two models' sources barely overlap: Jaccard 10% (n=100; 95% CI 7.4%–13%) per question.
  • Top-10 publishers hold 78% (n=100; 95% CI 75%–85%) of GPT-6.1 Sol's citation weight against 24% (n=100; 95% CI 23%–31%) of Claude Sonnet 5.5's (each answer counts equally, split over its cited URLs; the interval leans upward because the top 10 is re-picked in each resample).
  • Time-sensitive answers with no dated source under a year old: GPT-6.1 Sol 43% (49/114; 95% CI 31%–55%); Claude Sonnet 5.5 12% (14/114; 95% CI 5.8%–20%).

Date coverage 89% (745/841; 95% CI 86%–91%); dates checked on held-out pages: within 7 days 67% (18/27; 95% CI 48%–81%) (agent-checked, owner-approved). Sourcing only: no claim about medical accuracy.

Buyers

  • Publishers (health bodies, charities, NHS trusts, comms teams): are we cited, for which questions, who is cited instead, and what would help.
  • AI labs: sourcing quality by model and topic: concentration, staleness and support, measured the same way across models.
  • Agencies and researchers: a reproducible panel with error bars, not screenshots.

Delivery

  • Re-runs of a frozen question set (started by hand today; scheduling is planned); a run manifest pins models, code and config.
  • JSON results (format v1) and per-publisher cards; a static dashboard; a domain lookup (library function today, demo page planned).
  • Planned for v0.1.0: threshold checks, so a run fails in CI when a tracked metric crosses its bound.
  • Every share and rate ships with its count and 95% interval.

Pricing logic

Unit of value: one tracked question set × one model × one run, delivered with intervals. Cost scales with questions × models × runs (this study: 400 answers for $15.03 of API spend).

Limits

  • Directional: one country, 2 models, 100 questions per study.
  • Dates only from what pages state; unreadable pages are counted, never guessed.
