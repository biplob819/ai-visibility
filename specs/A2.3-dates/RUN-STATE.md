## Known limits
- The DAT-04 snapshots are normalised trims (date-bearing JSON-LD objects re-serialised, unrelated metas and markup dropped), so they test the resolver's choices, not raw-page parsing; the real-page shapes that broke parsing (no `@context`, control characters, nested images) are pinned by synthetic tests instead. DAT-04's labels were confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-16 (OWNER D-46); `test_the_labels_are_owner_confirmed` pins it.
- A site-wide footer like "Site last updated: <today>" is a visible-text `modified` candidate with no plausibility check, as §7.3 is written. More than 200,000 sitemap entries are cut. The `strict=False` on the first JSON-LD parse is masked by the non-strict fallback.
- A fetch-day `article:published_time` is not stamp-checked (§7.3 covers modified only); on a page whose real modified date is older it would reject that date as `modified_before_published`. A reference-list line such as "Published 12 March 2001" is a visible-text published candidate and the earliest wins. A sitemap that is too large or keeps answering 5xx is not cached and is fetched again each run, within the 3-request budget. `CreativeWork` microdata (GeneratePress, Astra) is not read, and RDFa reads every schema.org object on the page. The fail-safe `PageMeta` drops a page's candidates (unreachable in a 60,000-case fuzz). Closing tests: not written.
- A related-page card that sets its "Last reviewed" in a span of its own reads as a review line, and so does a label prefix in its own span (`<span>Media</span> last reviewed: …`, `<span>Bob</span> reviewed on …`): the line rule cannot tell a card's or prefix's span from a byline's. Numeric visible dates (`Last reviewed 10/05/2022`) are ambiguous and dropped, as D-45 rules. `parse_date` keeps the written date of `2021-05-01junk`. An unrelated JSON-LD object nested 50,000 deep drops that block's dates (other sources kept).
